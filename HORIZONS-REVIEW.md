# Horizons — what is shown, and the calls that are yours

Horizons (`/read/<n>/horizons/`) recites a surah over the signs it names. The
world it shows is drawn by the browser as the recitation plays; nothing is
filmed, nothing is downloaded, and nothing is heard but the reciter. Which word
is which sign is worked out for all 6,236 ayat by `npm run gen:horizons` from
the corpus's morphology, and corrected by hand in
`src/content/horizons/direction.txt`, one line to a point.

The section letters are for answering by, as with the introductions. Your
rulings are quoted in each section, with what was done about them.

## A. What is never drawn

> *"Your position of images and drawing is fine anything you draw is as you
> said but videography of filming or digital photography is allowed … videos
> showing these animals is fine fish swimming thats fine as its natural."*

1. **Nothing with a soul is drawn.** No person, animal, bird or fish — not in
   a story, not in the distance. Plants are drawn: palms, olives, pines,
   cypresses, fields, grass. **Kept as it was.**
2. **Filmed creatures are allowed.** Real footage or photographs of the
   natural world — birds in flight, fish swimming, plants growing — may be
   shown where the ayah names them. **Not added yet:** it means downloading
   clips, which I will not do without your go-ahead on each one (file, source,
   size, and that it is free to use and has no people in it).
3. **Nothing of the unseen.** Not Allah, His Throne or Kursī, the angels, the
   jinn, Iblīs, the Day, the Garden, the Fire. When an ayah speaks of them the
   world falls back out of focus and the light of the lamp comes down over it,
   with the line *"Of the unseen — heard, not shown."* The land under a veiled
   stretch is also laid out bare — no palms, fields or streams — so nothing
   glimpsed as the veil closes or lifts could be taken for the Garden.
4. **Nothing re-enacted.** A story's setting may be shown as land — the sea
   Mūsā crossed is shown as a sea — but never its people, its miracle or its
   punishment as an event.
5. **Nothing heard but the recitation.** No music, no thunder, no wind —
   *"thunder and rain … you can show but no need to add the noise to it."*

## B. The unseen

How an ayah is veiled: from the first word for the unseen in it to its end.
The words are *jannah, nār, jahannam, al-jaḥīm, saʿīr, saqar, laẓā,
al-ḥuṭamah, al-hāwiyah, firdaws, ʿadn, zaqqūm, ghislīn, ḍarīʿ, salsabīl,
tasnīm, ḥūr, sundus, istabraq, arāʾik, al-khuld, al-qiyāmah, al-sāʿah,
al-ṣūr, al-ʿarsh, al-kursī, malak/malāʾikah, jinn/jānn, Iblīs, shayṭān,
al-rūḥ, al-ākhirah,* "the Day of …" (of Judgement, Decision, Gathering,
Reckoning…), and *yawmaʾidhin*. Then whole passages with no such word are
veiled by hand: the Hour's signs (81:1–14, 82:1–5, 84:1–15, 99, 101…), and
descriptions of the Garden that do not name it (55:46–78, 56:10–40, 76:5–22…).
`direction.txt` has the full list, surah by surah.

Where a word for the Garden or the Fire is used of this world — an orchard in
a parable, a fire kindled in the cold — the ayah is marked as of this world
and the word is a sign (2:265–266, 6:99, 18:32–42, 36:34, 36:80, 56:71–73…).

**Your rulings, and what was done:**

- **B1. Āyat al-Kursī (2:255)** — *"show Allah's might and majestic creation
  instead."* No longer veiled, and nothing is shown for the Kursī. From "the
  heavens" the camera is high over a great range of mountains in the last of
  the night, stars still out; it glides on over ridge after ridge as the sky
  pales, and the sun comes up over the peaks part way through the ayah. At
  "extends over the heavens and the earth" it rises out past the air: the
  earth's curve below, its thin blue rim, the sun over it, and the stars,
  for "their preservation tires Him not. And He is the Most High, the Most
  Great."
- **B2. The Night Journey (17:1)** — *"a top down moving along the desert at
  night till you get to Jerusalem and the golden dome mosque and then as you
  start to rise it shows space … you NEVER show the Prophet."* No longer
  veiled. Land and sky only: no figure of any kind, no Burāq, nothing that
  moves but the camera. At the opening words, Makkah by night from above:
  al-Masjid al-Ḥarām among its mountains — the white mataf, the Kaʿbah dark
  with its band of gold, the colonnade, the minarets — and the city's lights.
  At "from al-Masjid al-Ḥarām to al-Masjid al-Aqṣā", the road north: the
  desert by moonlight streaming past below. At al-Aqṣā the camera comes down
  from the south, low, over al-Masjid al-Aqṣā towards the Dome of the Rock:
  the platform's lamps, its olives, pines and cypresses, its four minarets,
  the Dome of the Chain, the walls, the old city's lit lanes. At "to show
  him of Our signs" it rises straight up, looking back down on Jerusalem as
  the country spreads out under it — the real country: the Dead Sea, the
  coast and its cities, the wilderness of Judah dark between — and at "the
  Hearing" it is in orbit, Cyprus in the moonlit sea and the curve of the
  earth ahead.
- **B3. The Throne** (7:54, 10:3, 13:2, 25:59, 32:4, 57:4) — *"don't even
  attempt."* Nothing at all happens at the word: not a veil, not a breath of
  one. The creation those ayat speak of goes on being shown around it.
- **B4–B8** — *"anything of the unseen don't do it but something like thunder
  and rain … you can show but no need to add the noise."* As before: the
  storm of 13:13 is shown, silent, and the angels in it are not drawn; the
  stars of 67:5 are shown and its devils and Blaze veiled; the splitting of
  the moon (54:1–8), the smoke of 41:11 and of 44:10–16 stay veiled.
- **B9. 24:35, the parable of the light**, is kept as it was: it shows the
  parable's own image — the niche, the lamp, the glass "as if a brilliant
  star" — which is the site's emblem already; it does not picture "His
  light". Say if you would rather it were veiled.

## C. The stories

> *"The stories you can show landmarks or how it might have looked judging
> from archaeological findings."*

Shown as the land and sky they happened in — and now, where one stands, the
place itself:

- Ibrāhīm watching the star, the moon and the sun rise and set (6:76–78): the
  night sky, moonrise, sunrise.
- Maryam at the palm with the stream beneath her (19:23–26): a palm and water.
- Dhū al-Qarnayn finding the sun setting in a dark spring (18:86): a sunset
  over water; the sun rising (18:90).
- The junction of the two seas, where Mūsā met al-Khiḍr (18:60): two seas.
- Nūḥ's flood (11:40–44, 54:11–14): rain, springs, a storm at sea — then the
  sky withholding and a mountain (al-Jūdī). No ship.
- ʿĀd's wind (41:16, 54:19, 69:6): a gale over dunes. The people "like hollow
  palm trunks" are a simile and are *not* drawn as palms (D).
- Pharaoh's drowning (2:50, 10:90, 20:77–78): the sea, calm or rough. Nothing
  of the drowning.
- The Night Journey (17:1): al-Masjid al-Ḥarām and the Noble Sanctuary, as
  B2 describes.

**Two things to know about the landmarks.** Both are drawn as they stand
today, not as they stood on the night of the Journey: the Ḥaram with its
present colonnades and minarets, and the Noble Sanctuary with the Dome of the
Rock, built some seventy years after it. Drawing them as they are is what
makes them recognisable; drawing them as they were would mean reconstructing
from archaeology, which I have not attempted. And the map of the Levant seen
from orbit is drawn from the real coastlines, seas and cities, simplified —
it is a likeness at that height, not a survey.

## D. Similes, not signs

The film does not take these literally: waves "like mountains" (11:42),
"mountains" of cloud (24:43), "as if hollow palm trunks" (69:7, 54:20),
"like dry twigs" (54:31), "eaten straw" (105:5), "Pharaoh of the stakes"
(38:12, 89:10), "fortified towers" (4:78), the mustard seed of deeds (31:16,
21:47), three darknesses of the womb (39:6), "a valley without cultivation"
(14:37 — shown as bare rock), the poets in every valley (26:225), "as if he
had fallen from the sky" (22:31), the demands made of the Prophet ﷺ (17:90–93).

## E. Coral, and the creatures

"Pearls and coral" (55:22): the camera goes under the surface into sunlit
water with glints of light. Coral is an animal, so none is drawn. The bee
(16:68), the camels (88:17), the birds (67:19, 24:41), the cattle and the
horses are named in the words on screen and not drawn; where the ayah says
"look", the camera slows and looks — at the sky, the mountains, the earth
the same ayat go on to name. Under your ruling in A, these are the places
where real footage could later be shown — birds in flight for 67:19, the bee
for 16:68, fish for the seas.

## F. Darkness

*Ẓulumāt* is most often a likeness — "out of the darknesses into the light" —
so it darkens the world for a while and lifts, rather than bringing on night.
The night itself comes only with the words for night.

## G. The sky tonight

The film begins at the hour it is where the viewer is, under the moon's real
phase that day. A word for the sun at midnight brings on the dawn — the
film's day only runs forward — and a sign of the land, water or what grows,
named at night, brings the morning. The one liberty with the sky: a sun or
moon that is named is lowered and turned into the frame. By night the
moonlight is drawn silver-blue, as the eye sees it, so that towns stand warm
against it.
